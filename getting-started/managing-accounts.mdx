The Accounts page is where you manage which customer accounts are in Cora and who owns them. You can import accounts from your CRM, configure ownership sync so the right team members are mapped to each account, filter your view by ownership, and delete accounts you no longer track.

## Importing Accounts from Your CRM

Once your CRM (Salesforce or HubSpot) is connected, you can import accounts into Cora. Filters let you control which accounts are brought in.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the Import Dialog">
    1. Navigate to the **Accounts** page.
    2. Click **New Account**.
    3. Click **Sync from Salesforce** or **Sync from HubSpot**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add Filters">
    Add one or more filters to select which accounts to import. You can filter on any available field from your CRM, for example, by industry, region, lifecycle stage, record type, or account tier. Supported operators include equals, not equals, greater/less than, contains, and in/not in a list.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Preview">
    Click **Preview** to review results across three tabs:

    - **New to import** — CRM accounts that match your filters and are not yet in Cora
    - **Already in Cora** — accounts already imported that still match the filters
    - **No longer matching** — accounts already imported that no longer match the filters

    Each tab shows up to 25 sample results. If there are accounts no longer matching, a warning banner will indicate how many will be deleted if they have no activity.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Import">
    Click **Request Import** to begin.

    The import runs in the background. New accounts/companies will be created as accounts in Cora, or linked to existing ones if a match is found.

    Importing saves your filters and keeps accounts in sync.

    If an account's name changes in your CRM, Cora will update it automatically.

    If an account no longer matches your filters, it will be deleted from Cora.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Account Ownership Sync

Ownership sync maps CRM fields to Cora team roles, so the right team members are associated with each account. This powers the ownership filters on the Accounts and Signals tabs.

### Setting up ownership sync

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open ownership sync">
    1. Go to **Accounts**.
    2. Click **New Account**.
    3. Click **Sync from Salesforce** or **Sync from HubSpot**.
    4. Open the **Account Ownership** tab.
    5. Turn on **Enable ownership sync**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add a role">
    Click **Add a role** and pick a role. Each role gets its own card. The roles are **Account Owner**, **Manager**, **Technical Account Manager**, **Customer Success Manager**, **Account Executive**, **Client Delivery Lead**, **Change Activation Partner**, **Technical Implementation Manager**, **Account Manager**, and **Client Partner**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add lines">
    Each line reads **The person in** or **The manager of**, then a CRM field that holds a user.

    **The manager of** is Salesforce only. It uses the Salesforce user hierarchy, so Cora assigns the reporting manager of the person in that field.

    Click **Add another line for** the role to add another line. The button includes the role name, for example **Add another line for Account Owner**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save">
    Click **Save**. **Saved. Account ownership will sync on the next data refresh.** appears only when there are no warnings. If a source column has values and none match a CRM user, **Save** still succeeds, the usual success confirmation is not shown, and Cora warns that the line will not assign anyone while existing owners stay until a line matches.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### How lines work

The tab shows: **Lines run top to bottom. Changes apply the next time accounts refresh, and owners who no longer match are removed then.**

- Each line after the first joins the line above with **Otherwise** (**Only if nobody was found above**) or **Also** (**Add this person as another owner too**).
- Each line shows **Only when all of these match**, including when it has no conditions yet, and the condition rows stay open. Add a condition with **Add another condition**. All conditions on a line must match. The operators are the same as the import filters.
- **Skip if this person is inactive in Salesforce** passes the account to the next line when that user is inactive. On a **The manager of** line, the label is **Skip if the manager is inactive in Salesforce**, and Cora checks the manager instead. Both options are Salesforce only.
- Reorder a line with **Move line up** and **Move line down**.

### Example

On an **Account Owner** card, one chain looks like this:

1. **The person in** **Customer Success Manager**, **Only when** **Implementation Status** equals **Success**, with **Skip if this person is inactive in Salesforce**.
2. **Otherwise**, **The person in** **Implementation Manager**, with **Skip if this person is inactive in Salesforce**.
3. **Otherwise**, **The manager of** **Implementation Manager**.

Cora tries the lines from top to bottom. **Otherwise** runs only when no earlier line found someone.

### Notes

<Note>
  **does not equal** and **is not one of** do not match accounts where the field is empty.
</Note>

<Warning>
  If a condition field no longer exists in your CRM, the line shows `{field} no longer exists in {CRM}. Pick another field or remove this condition.` **Save** is blocked only while ownership sync is on and the field list has loaded, and the button then reads **Fix 1 problem to save** or **Fix N problems to save**. Turning ownership sync off lets **Save** succeed without changing the condition. If the field list has not loaded, **Save** is not blocked and the dialog warns `Couldn't verify that every condition field still exists in {CRM}, so this was saved without that check.` If a saved field disappears later, Cora leaves that role unchanged on refresh rather than reassigning owners.
</Warning>

<Note>
  Existing setups open as lines joined by **Also**, which behaves the same as before.
</Note>

Ownership syncs automatically on each data refresh. Cora resolves users by matching the email address associated with the CRM user to a Cora user account.

If a CRM owner does not yet have a Cora account, one is automatically created so they appear on the account team. These users appear on the team list but have no app access.

## Filtering by Ownership

Once ownership sync is configured, the Accounts and Signals tabs display a filter with three options:

- **All Accounts** — shows every account (default)
- **My Accounts** — shows only accounts where you have the Account Owner role
- **My Team's Accounts** — shows accounts where you have the Manager role (that is, accounts owned by your direct reports)

The "My Team's Accounts" option only appears for users who have a Manager role on at least one account.

## Adding Accounts Manually

You can also add accounts individually without importing from a CRM:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Add a new account manually">
    1. Navigate to the **Accounts** page.
    2. Click **New Account**.
    3. Enter the account details and click **Save**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Deleting Accounts

<Steps>
  <Step title="Select the accounts">
    1. Navigate to the **Accounts** page.
    2. Use the checkboxes to select one or more accounts.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm">
    Click **Delete**, then confirm in the dialog.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  Deleting an account is permanent. The account and its data are not archived, and there is no undo.
</Warning>

### Who Can Delete an Account

You can delete an account if you created it, or if you are an admin. An operator who did not create an account cannot delete it.

What happens when you confirm depends on your role:

- **Admins** delete the account together with everything attached to it. The confirmation dialog lists what will be removed before you continue.
- **Everyone else** can only delete an account that is empty. If the account still holds data, Cora refuses the delete and tells you what it found. Remove those items first, or ask an admin to delete the account for you.

<Tip>
  When you delete several accounts at once, Cora handles each one independently. If some are refused, the rest are still deleted, and the notification lists each failure with its reason.
</Tip>
